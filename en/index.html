How to make a finding chart

01

Set the field

Enter RA, Dec and the field width in arcminutes. The target name is used only as a label.

02

Select “Find stars”

Enter coordinates and field width. The site estimates target brightness and groups candidates by magnitude difference: ±1, up to ±3, and a reserve up to ±4. APASS V has priority within the window. Inspect the table and the DESI Legacy Surveys or DSS2 image, assign REF/CHECK after image validation, then download the finding chart.

03

Review and save

Numbers on the finding chart match the table. You can remove unwanted markers. For a paper, enable monochrome and download SVG or PNG; the CSV contains all magnitudes.

TIP · SIMILAR BRIGHTNESS

Prefer stars closest in magnitude to your target in the same band and system. For an 18 mag target, prefer similarly bright stars over 12 mag stars. This guides your final choice: the site shows all candidates found without magnitude limits. Check saturation, colour and stability in your images.

PHOTOMETRY · WHAT THE NUMBERS MEAN
What is a photometric catalogue?
A photometric catalogue supplies measured stellar brightnesses and uncertainties in defined passbands. You do not need to choose one: the site checks APASS and Pan-STARRS1 automatically and labels every magnitude with its source. Catalogues differ in coverage, depth, passbands and systems. A missing measurement in one band does not hide the other measurements of that star.

Observing in R or I? Check the filter system
Sloan r′ ≠ Cousins R꜀ and Sloan i′ ≠ Cousins I꜀; PS1 rₚ₁ and iₚ₁ are also different. Establish your filter’s exact system first. For Cousins, prefer measured R꜀/I꜀ standards, such as a suitable AAVSO VSP sequence or Landolt/Stetson fields.
A catalogue transformation must match the input system, stellar colours and stellar type. Jordi et al. (2006) also show metallicity dependence. Changing an AB zero point to Vega alone does not change the passband.
Lupton (2005) examples for SDSS → Cousins, not universal conversions:
R꜀ = r − 0.2936(r − i) − 0.1439
I꜀ = r − 1.2444(r − i) − 0.3820
Lupton reports scatters of 0.0072 and 0.0078 mag, respectively. Result uncertainties must account for input errors, covariance, fit scatter and system-transfer uncertainty. Do not apply these coefficients directly to PS1. For APASS Sloan, verify system consistency and applicability too.
The generator displays native catalogue bands and does not automatically transform to R꜀/I꜀. In the curated IC 1495 field, R꜀ is labelled as a provisional estimate from APASS r′/i′. Calibrating your instrumental magnitudes needs separate coefficients for the telescope, filter and camera.

03 / TRANSPARENT BY DESIGN
How stars are selected
A finding chart should be understandable and reproducible.
Here is what happens when you build a finding chart.
Photometric quality
The site checks magnitude uncertainties, scatter across repeated measurements and quality flags. PS1 measurements brighter than the conservative saturation limits g/r/i/z/y = 14.5/15/15/14/13 mag are shown for reference only. Sources with large scatter in a usable unsaturated band require time-series review and are excluded from automatic recommendations.
APASS or Pan-STARRS?
APASS has priority for Johnson V because it measures V directly; its main survey covers roughly 10–17 mag. Pan-STARRS is useful for fainter targets in its native gₚ₁rₚ₁iₚ₁zₚ₁yₚ₁ bands. It does not supply Johnson V. Neither catalogue is a list of guaranteed constant standards.
How target brightness is estimated
Match the supplied coordinates to one APASS counterpart within 2″ or one PS1 counterpart within 1″; ambiguous associations are not used. A positive catalogue uncertainty up to 0.3 mag is allowed for a rough target estimate, not for comparison stars. APASS V is preferred, followed by rₚ₁, gₚ₁ and other available bands. Δm requires matching catalogue, band, system and zero point. Missing estimates are reported explicitly.
Start with the closest brightness matches. ±3–4 mag is a broad reserve: 4 mag corresponds to about a factor of 40 in flux. Check colour, saturation, S/N and an independent CHECK. Validating stability requires time series: assess scatter against the noise of stars of similar brightness, with a magnitude-dependent noise model.

01
Field geometry
RA accepts decimal degrees or hh:mm:ss; Dec is in degrees. A cone covers the square field diagonal plus a margin for neighbour searches. An exact TAN projection and square boundary are then applied.
02
Catalogue photometry
APASS DR9 provides B, V, g′, r′ and i′. Pan-STARRS1 DR1 provides g, r, i, z and y in the native PS1 system. Bands from different systems remain distinct; Johnson–Cousins transformations are not applied automatically.
03
Candidate screening
Required: an unambiguous Gaia counterpart, astrometric quality, isolation and at least one eligible photometric band. Gaia VARIABLE, VSX, ASAS-SN and ZTF associations, and PS1 variability flags exclude the source. Incomplete checks never produce new candidates.
04
Selection and export
Brightness tiers: |Δm| ≤ 1, 1–3, and a 3–4 mag reserve. With a target estimate, initial markers use APASS V within ±3, then other common bands, then the ±4 reserve if needed. Without an estimate, only APASS V catalogue priority applies. All candidates and all available bands remain in the table.
A candidate is not a validated standard. Gaia NOT_AVAILABLE does not mean CONSTANT. No association in the queried catalogues does not establish constancy in every band or on every timescale. Validate time series, saturation, blending and an independent CHECK star.
Selection criteria and observational validation
Automatic screening combines astrometric, isolation, photometric and catalogue variability checks. The criteria below describe the selection thresholds and the checks to perform using your observations.
At least one band must pass 0 < uncertainty ≤ 0.05 mag. For APASS with flag 0 this is the scatter of observations, not the error of the mean; ≥ 2 observed nights are required. PS1 requires ≥ 3 measurements, acceptable flags and an available scatter ≤ 0.05 mag. Large scatter in a usable unsaturated PS1 band holds the entire object for time-series review. This is a conservative tool threshold, not evidence of variability or constancy.
Variability: Gaia DR3, VSX (known and suspected variables), ASAS-SN II/366/catv2021, and confirmed and suspected ZTF sources from Chen et al. (2020). Variable-list matching radius: 3″. All VSX associations are conservatively excluded; V=2 requires manual review and does not assert confirmed variability. These are specific releases with limited coverage, not every existing catalogue. Query dates, tables and results are retained in JSON.
  • Automatic: unique Gaia association within 1″; RUWE < 1.4; parallax S/N ≥ 3 or proper-motion significance ≥ 3; Dup = 0; IPD multiple peaks ≤ 2%; no Gaia neighbour within 10″; target exclusion within 10″. Proper motion > 50 mas/yr requires manual epoch matching and is excluded automatically. These are conservative working thresholds, not evidence of constancy.
  • From images: saturation and linearity, S/N, neighbours in the aperture and sky annulus, seeing and detector-position effects; CHECK stability and leave-one-out tests of REF stars. Keep CHECK outside the REF ensemble.
  • Limits: proper motions are not propagated between epochs; verify cross-match radii. RUWE describes the astrometric solution, not brightness stability. Absence from VSX or a Gaia VARIABLE flag does not establish non-variability.
  • For AGN: comparison stars calibrate flux but do not remove seeing-dependent host-galaxy contamination. Use consistent apertures, control the PSF/background and assess the host contribution separately.
CHECK should closely match the target brightness; REF may be somewhat brighter if it remains linear and has adequate S/N. Comparing light-curve scatter at different brightnesses requires a photometric-noise model; equal noise cannot simply be assumed.
